## Pareto Efficiency

- Vilfredo Pareto; 1848–1923.
- A Pareto outcome allows no “wasted welfare”;
- i.e. the only way one person’s welfare can be improved is to lower another person’s welfare.

![Pareto efficiency diagram 1](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_28_0.png)
![Pareto efficiency diagram 2](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_28_1.png)

- A Pareto inefficient outcome means there remain unrealized mutual gains-to-trade.
- Any market outcome that achieves all possible gains-to-trade must be Pareto efficient.

![Pareto efficiency diagram 3](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_29_0.png)

- **Competitive equilibrium:**
  - all apartment renters value them at the market price pᵉ or more
  - all others value apartments at less than pᵉ
  - so no mutually beneficial trades remain
  - so the outcome is Pareto efficient.

- **Discriminatory Monopoly:**
  - assignment of apartments is the same as with the perfectly competitive market
  - so the discriminatory monopoly outcome is also Pareto efficient.

![Discriminatory monopoly Pareto efficiency diagram](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_31_0.png)

- **Monopoly:**
  - not all apartments are occupied
  - monopolist can increase profit by renting an empty apartment to someone who does not have an apartment before as long as the price he charges this person is not higher than this person’s reservation price.
  - so the monopoly outcome is Pareto inefficient.

### 2 Budgetary and Other Constraints on Choice

### Consumption Choice Sets

- A consumption choice set is the collection of all consumption choices available to the consumer.
- What constrains consumption choice?
  - Budgetary, time and other resource limitations.

![Consumption choice sets diagram](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_36_0.png)

### Budget Constraints

- A consumption bundle containing x₁ units of commodity 1, x₂ units of commodity 2 and so on up to xₙ units of commodity n is denoted by the vector (x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ).
- Commodity prices are p₁, p₂, ..., pₙ.

![Budget constraint diagram](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_37_0.png)

- Q: When is a consumption bundle (x₁, ..., xₙ) affordable at given prices p₁, ..., pₙ?
- A: It is affordable when p₁x₁ + ... + pₙxₙ ≤ m, where m is the consumer’s (disposable) income.
- All the bundles that are only just affordable form the consumer’s budget constraint. This is the set

{ (x₁,...,xₙ) | x₁ ≥ 0, ..., xₙ ≥ 0 and p₁x₁ + ... + pₙxₙ = m }.
