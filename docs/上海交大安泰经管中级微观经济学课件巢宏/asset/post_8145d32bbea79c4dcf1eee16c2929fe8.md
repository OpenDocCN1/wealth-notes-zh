
## Preference Relations

- **Comparing two different consumption bundles, x and y:**
  - **strict preference:** x is more preferred than y; x ≻ y.
  - **indifference:** x is exactly as preferred as y; x ∼ y.
  - **weak preference:** x is at least as preferred as y; x ≽ y.

- x ≽ y and y ≽ x imply x ∼ y.
- x ≽ y and (not y ≽ x) imply x ≻ y.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_84_0.png)

## Assumptions about Preference Relations

- **Completeness:** For any two bundles x and y it is always possible to make the statement that either x ≽ y or y ≽ x.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_85_0.png)

- **Reflexivity:** Any bundle x is always at least as preferred as itself; *i.e.* x ≽ x.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_86_0.png)

- **Transitivity:** If x is at least as preferred as y, and y is at least as preferred as z, then x is at least as preferred as z; *i.e.* x ≽ y and y ≽ z → x ≽ z.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_87_0.png)

## Indifference Curves

- Take a reference bundle x. The set of all bundles equally preferred to x is the indifference curve containing x.

## Extreme Cases of Indifference Curves; Perfect Substitutes

- If a consumer is willing to substitute one commodity for the other at a constant rate, then the commodities are perfect substitutes and only the total amount of the two commodities in bundles determines their preference rank-order.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_94_0.png)

Slopes are constant at −1.

Bundles in I₂ all have a total of 15 units and are strictly preferred to all bundles in I₁, which have a total of only 8 units in them.

## Extreme Cases of Indifference Curves; Perfect Complements

- If a consumer always consumes commodities 1 and 2 in fixed proportion (e.g. one-to-one), then the commodities are **perfect complements** and only the number of pairs of units of the two commodities determines the preference rank-order of bundles.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_97_0.png)

Each of **(5,5)**, **(5,9)** and **(9,5)** contains 5 pairs so each is equally preferred.

## Indifference Curves for Discrete Commodities

- A commodity is infinitely divisible (or continuous) if it can be acquired in any quantity; e.g. water or cheese.
- A commodity is discrete if it comes in unit lumps of 1, 2, 3, ... and so on; e.g. aircraft, ships and refrigerators.
