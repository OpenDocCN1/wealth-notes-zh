## Two-Part Tariffs

◆ **The monopolist maximizes its profit when using a two-part tariff by setting its per unit price p₂ at marginal cost and setting its lump-sum fee p₁ equal to Consumers’ Surplus.**

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1532_0.png)

## Two-Part Tariffs

+   ♦ A profit-maximizing two-part tariff gives an efficient market outcome in which the monopolist obtains as profit the total of all gains-to-trade.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1533_0.png)

## Monopolistic Competition

+   - Rare to see pure monopoly, other firms may compete by producing similar products.
+   - Implications:
+     - The monopolist still has market power – demand slopes down
+     - Competition from similar products drives profit to zero.
+     - To minimize competition, a firm tries to differentiate product.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1535_0.png)

## Differentiating Products

+   - In many markets the commodities traded are very close, but not perfect, substitutes.
+   - *E.g.,* the markets for T-shirts, watches, cars, and cookies.
+   - Each individual supplier thus has some slight “monopoly power.”
+   - What does an equilibrium look like for such a market?

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1536_0.png)

## Differentiating Products

+   - Free entry ⇒ zero profits for each seller.
+   - Profit-maximization ⇒ MR = MC for each seller.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1538_0.png)
