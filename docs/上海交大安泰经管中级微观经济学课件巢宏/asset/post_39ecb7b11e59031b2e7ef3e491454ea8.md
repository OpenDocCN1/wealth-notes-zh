## Collusion & Punishment Strategies

PV^M = $56 + $56/(1+r) + $56/(1+r)^2 + ⋯ = $ (1+r)56 / r.

PV^CH = $65 + $46/(1+r) + $46/(1+r)^2 + ⋯ = $65 + $46 / r.

# So the cartel will be stable if

(1+r)56 / r > 65 + 46 / r ⇒ r < 10/9 ⇒ 1/(1+r) > 9/19.

## The Order of Play

- So far it has been assumed that firms choose their output levels simultaneously.
- The competition between the firms is then a simultaneous play game in which the output levels are the strategic variables.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1658_0.png)

## The Order of Play

- What if firm 1 chooses its output level first and then firm 2 responds to this choice?
- Firm 1 is then a leader. Firm 2 is a follower.
- The competition is a sequential game in which the output levels are the strategic variables.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1659_0.png)

## The Order of Play

- Such games are von Stackelberg games.
- Is it better to be the leader?
- Or is it better to be the follower?

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1660_0.png)

## Stackelberg Games

- ◆ A: Choose y₂ = R₂(y₁).
- ◆ Firm 1 knows this and so perfectly anticipates firm 2’s reaction to any y₁ chosen by firm 1.

## Stackelberg Games

- ◆ This makes the leader’s profit function

$\Pi_1^s(y_1) = p(y_1 + R_2(y_1))y_1 - c_1(y_1).$

## Stackelberg Games

- This makes the leader’s profit function
  Π₁ˢ(y₁) = p(y₁ + R₂(y₁))y₁ − c₁(y₁).
- The leader chooses y₁ to maximize its profit.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1665_0.png)

## Stackelberg Games

◆ A: Yes. The leader could choose its Cournot-Nash output level, knowing that the follower would then also choose its C-N output level. The leader’s profit would then be its C-N profit. But the leader does not have to do this, so its profit must be at least as large as its C-N profit.

## Stackelberg Games; An Example

- ♦ The market inverse demand function is p = 60 - y_T. The firms’ cost functions are c₁(y₁) = y₁² and c₂(y₂) = 15y₂ + y₂².
- ♦ Firm 2 is the follower. Its reaction function is

y₂ = R₂(y₁) = (45 − y₁)/4.

## Stackelberg Games; An Example

The leader’s profit function is therefore

Π₁ˢ(y₁) = (60 − y₁ − R₂(y₁))y₁ − y₁²
= (60 − y₁ − (45 − y₁)/4) y₁ − y₁²
= (195/4) y₁ − (7/4) y₁².

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1669_0.png)

## Stackelberg Games; An Example

**The leader’s profit function is therefore**

$$\Pi_1^S(y_1)=(60-y_1-R_2(y_1))y_1-y_1^2$$
$$=(60-y_1-\frac{45-y_1}{4})y_1-y_1^2$$
$$=\frac{195}{4}y_1-\frac{7}{4}y_1^2.$$

**For a profit-maximum for firm 1,**

$$\frac{195}{4}-\frac{7}{2}y_1\Rightarrow y_1^S=13.9.$$
