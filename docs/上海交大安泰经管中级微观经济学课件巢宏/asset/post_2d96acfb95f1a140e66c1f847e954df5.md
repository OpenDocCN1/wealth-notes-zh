
## Markup Pricing

+   ◆ Markup pricing: Output price is the marginal cost of production times a “markup.”
+   ◆ How big is a monopolist’s markup and how does it change with the own-price elasticity of demand?

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1432_0.png)

## Markup Pricing

$$p(y^*)\left[1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right]=k \quad\Rightarrow\quad p(y^*)=\frac{k}{1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}=\frac{k\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}$$

is the monopolist’s price. The markup is

$$\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}.$$

## A Profits Tax Levied on a Monopoly

+   - A profits tax levied at rate t reduces profit from Π(y*) to (1-t)Π(y*).
+   - Q: How is after-tax profit, (1-t)Π(y*), maximized?
+   - A: By maximizing before-tax profit, Π(y*).

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1437_0.png)

## A Profits Tax Levied on a Monopoly

+   - A profits tax levied at rate t reduces profit from Π(y*) to (1-t)Π(y*).
+   - Q: How is after-tax profit, (1-t)Π(y*), maximized?
+   - A: By maximizing before-tax profit, Π(y*).
+   - So a profits tax has no effect on the monopolist’s choices of output level, output price, or demands for inputs.
+   - I.e. the profits tax is a neutral tax.

## Quantity Tax Levied on a Monopolist

+   - A quantity tax of $t/output unit raises the marginal cost of production by $t.
+   - So the tax reduces the profit-maximizing output level, causes the market price to rise, and input demands to fall.
+   - The quantity tax is distortionary.

## Quantity Tax Levied on a Monopolist

+   - Can a monopolist “pass” all of a $t quantity tax to the consumers?
+   - Suppose the marginal cost of production is constant at $k/output unit and the monopolist is facing a constant-elasticity demand curve.
+   - With no tax, the monopolist’s price is $p(y^*) = \frac{k\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}$.

## Quantity Tax Levied on a Monopolist

+   - The tax increases marginal cost to $(k+t)$/output unit, changing the profit-maximizing price to
+     $$p(y^t)=\frac{(k+t)\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}.$$

+   - The amount of the tax paid by buyers is
+     $$p(y^t)-p(y^*).$$

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1445_0.png)

## Quantity Tax Levied on a Monopolist

p(yᵗ) − p(y*) = (k + t)ε / (1 + ε) − kε / (1 + ε) = tε / (1 + ε)

is the amount of the tax passed on to buyers. E.g. if ε = -2, the amount of the tax passed on is 2t.

Because ε < -1, ε / (1 + ε) > 1 and so the monopolist passes on to consumers more than the tax!

## The Inefficiency of Monopoly

+   - A market is Pareto efficient if it achieves the maximum possible total gains-to-trade.
+   - Otherwise a market is Pareto inefficient.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1447_0.png)

## The Inefficiency of Monopoly

The efficient output level yᵉ satisfies p(y) = MC(y).
