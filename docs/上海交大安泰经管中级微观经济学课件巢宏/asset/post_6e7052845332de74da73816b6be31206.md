
## Fixed Inputs and Economic Rent

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1393_0.png)

**The firm’s economic profit is zero.**

$F$ is the payment to the owner of the fixed input (the license).

- Economic rent is the payment for an input that is in excess of the minimum payment required to have that input supplied.
- Each license essentially costs zero to supply, so the long-run economic rent paid to the license owner is the firm’s long-run fixed cost.

## 24 Monopoly

### Pure Monopoly

- A monopolized market has a single seller.
- The monopolist’s demand curve is the (downward sloping) market demand curve.
- So the monopolist can alter the market price by adjusting its output level.

`$/output unit`; $p(y)$

Higher output $y$ causes a lower market price, $p(y)$.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1398_0.png)

Output Level, $y$

### Why Monopolies?

### What causes monopolies?

- a legal fiat; e.g. US Postal Service
- a patent; e.g. a new drug
- sole ownership of a resource; e.g. a toll highway
- formation of a cartel; e.g. OPEC
- large economies of scale; e.g. local utility companies

### Profit-Maximization

- Suppose that the monopolist seeks to maximize its economic profit,

$$\Pi(y)=p(y)y-c(y).$$

- What output level $y^*$ maximizes profit?

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1400_0.png)

At the profit-maximizing output level $y^*$,

$$\frac{d\Pi(y)}{dy}=\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(p(y)y\bigr)-\frac{dc(y)}{dy}=0$$

so, for $y=y^*$,

$$\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(p(y)y\bigr)=\frac{dc(y)}{dy}.$$

### Marginal Revenue

Marginal revenue is the rate-of-change of revenue as the output level $y$ increases:

$$MR(y)=\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(p(y)y\bigr)=p(y)+y\frac{dp(y)}{dy}.$$

$\frac{dp(y)}{dy}$ is the slope of the market inverse demand function, so $\frac{dp(y)}{dy}<0$. Therefore

$$MR(y)=p(y)+y\frac{dp(y)}{dy}<p(y)$$

for $y>0$.

E.g. if $p(y)=a-by$, then

$$R(y)=p(y)y=ay-by^2$$

and so

$$MR(y)=a-2by<a-by=p(y)$$

for $y>0$.

### Marginal Cost

Marginal cost is the rate-of-change of total cost as the output level $y$ increases:

$$MC(y)=\frac{dc(y)}{dy}.$$

E.g. if $c(y)=F+\alpha y+\beta y^2$, then

$$MC(y)=\alpha+2\beta y.$$
