
## Collusion & Punishment Strategies

- (iii) What is the profit the cheater earns in each period after it first cheats?
  - This depends upon the punishment inflicted upon the cheater by the other firm.
  - Suppose the other firm punishes by forever not cooperating with the cheater.
  - What are the firms’ profits in the noncooperative C-N equilibrium?
    - \(p(y_T)=24-y_T\), \(c_1(y_1)=y_1^2\), \(c_2(y_2)=y_2^2\).
    - Given \(y_2\), firm 1’s profit function is
      \[
      \pi_1(y_1;y_2)=(24-y_1-y_2)y_1-y_1^2.
      \]
    - The value of \(y_1\) that is firm 1’s best response to \(y_2\) solves
      \[
      \frac{\partial \pi_1}{\partial y_1}=24-4y_1-y_2=0 \Rightarrow y_1=R_1(y_2)=\frac{24-y_2}{4}.
      \]
    - Similarly, \(y_2=R_2(y_1)=\frac{24-y_1}{4}\).
    - The C-N equilibrium \((y_1^*,y_2^*)\) solves \(y_1=R_1(y_2)\) and \(y_2=R_2(y_1)\), so \(y_1^*=y_2^*=4.8\).
    - So each firm’s profit in the C-N equilibrium is
      \[
      \pi_1^*=\pi_2^*=(14.4)(4.8)-4.8^2\approx \$46
      \]
      each period.

- (ii) What is the profit a cheater earns in the first period in which it cheats?
  - Firm 1 cheats on firm 2 by producing the quantity \(y_1^{CH}\) that maximizes firm 1’s profit given that firm 2 continues to produce \(y_2^M=4\). What is the value of \(y_1^{CH}\)?
  - \(y_1^{CH}=R_1(y_2^M)=\frac{24-y_2^M}{4}=\frac{24-4}{4}=5\).
  - Firm 1’s profit in the period in which it cheats is therefore
    \[
    \pi_1^{CH}=(24-5-1)(5)-5^2=\$65.
    \]

- To determine if such a cartel can be stable we need to know 3 things:
  - (i) What is each firm’s per period profit in the cartel? **$56.**
  - (ii) What is the profit a cheater earns in the first period in which it cheats? **$65.**
  - (iii) What is the profit the cheater earns in each period after it first cheats? **$46.**

- Each firm’s periodic discount factor is \(1/(1+r)\).
- The present-value of firm 1’s profits if it does not cheat is
  \[
  PV^M = \$56 + \frac{\$56}{1+r} + \frac{\$56}{(1+r)^2} + \cdots = \frac{(1+r)56}{r}.
  \]
