## Marginal Cost Function

◆ The firm’s total cost function is

\[
c(\mathbf{y}) = F + c_v(\mathbf{y})
\]

and the fixed cost \(F\) does not change with the output level \(\mathbf{y}\), so

\[
MC(\mathbf{y}) = \frac{\partial c_v(\mathbf{y})}{\partial \mathbf{y}} = \frac{\partial c(\mathbf{y})}{\partial \mathbf{y}}.
\]

◆ MC is the slope of both the variable cost and the total cost functions.

![](img/e3f3f402f74cf453caf4596d2d7d7398_1184_0.png)

## Marginal and Variable Cost Functions

◆ Since MC(y) is the derivative of cᵥ(y), cᵥ(y) must be the integral of MC(y). That is,

$$MC(y)=\frac{\partial c_v(y)}{\partial y}$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad c_v(y)=\int_0^y MC(z)\,dz.$$
