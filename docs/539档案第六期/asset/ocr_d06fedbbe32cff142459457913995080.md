# PROTEIN! Why protein answers a problem of teen-agers

![](bbox=[0.268, 0.357, 0.78, 0.514])

In this land of plenty, a surprising percentage of our teen-agers suffers from malnutrition. Trouble is, they're filling up on the wrong foods, say doctors, dietitians and nutritionists.

They agree that diets containing ample protein are especially important during the growth period. For without adequate protein, the young body doesn't have what it takes to develop properly. And emotional disturbances—the sudden storms followed by listlessness—can often be traced to this lack.

![](bbox=[0.272, 0.677, 0.38, 0.779])

How does protein work? To put it simply, the body is made up of millions of microscopic cells, each a tiny chemical factory manufacturing what the body needs. But before there can be any production, a plant must have raw materials. Proteins are these materials.

When is protein needed most? At breakfast. That's when the body has gone 12–14 hours without food. It's also the meal teen-agers are most likely to skip.

Recognizing this problem, Kellogg's worked with two leading Universities to come up with an answer. They found it in Special K—a new, ready-to-eat protein cereal. A serving with milk provides more high-quality protein than any other leading cereal—hot or cold.

Now teen-agers can get a good share of their vital protein requirements when they need it most—at breakfast. And the kind in Special K is unusually inviting to eat—a fact teen-agers, especially, will appreciate.
