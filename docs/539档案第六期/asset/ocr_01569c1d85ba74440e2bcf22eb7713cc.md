"What in the world will he get into next?"

(Facts about childhood's greatest enemy—accidents)

For a little boy or girl, everything in the world is interesting. But in finding out "the why of things," a great many youngsters are hurt or crippled or killed.

Studies prove that most childhood accidents need never happen. For example, deaths from accidental poisoning could be drastically cut by observing these simple precautions:

Store all drugs in a locked closet or cabinet.
Destroy all left-over medicines prescribed for temporary use. Don't throw them into a wastebasket where a child might find them.
Store all disinfectants, insecticides, furni-

ture polishes, bleaches, metal cleaners, lye, ammonia and acid out of the reach of children.

Keep all potentially harmful substances in their original containers. Don't transfer them to unlabeled containers, particularly those meant to hold food or beverages.

Read all labels carefully, and follow warning directions to the letter.

Find out if there's a Poison Control Center near you. These centers are ever ready to provide your physician with quick identification of poisonous ingredients, and directions for emergency, life-saving treatment.
