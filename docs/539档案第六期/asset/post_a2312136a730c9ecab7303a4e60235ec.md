
# 你家的车上是否“安全线”？

下次，你在车上的时候，检查一下你的车或窗户玻璃裸露的边缘上，是否能看到一条独特的线？如果能，很高兴你的车上安装了这样的玻璃。

你看到的实际上是夹在 2 块玻璃之间的一层坚实，完全透明的塑料材质。这层玻璃窗实际上是一个“塑料玻璃三明治”。这就是夹层安全玻璃。

你看不到挡风玻璃的边缘，你也不需要看到。美国制造的汽车防风玻璃，用的都是夹层安全玻璃。大多数汽车侧窗玻璃，还有门玻璃也采用了夹层安全玻璃。下面我们看看夹层安全玻璃是如何保护你的！

Item: From four separate communities come reports of a "rush" on glasses by teen-age girls. Not all need them, but they all say glasses make them look sophisticated and impress their friends.

# What’s happened
to Mr. Bell’s
invention?

![](img/8cffddf1c8314e0aa1e8e826db1d033a_116_0.png)

Eighty years ago Alexander Graham Bell invented the telephone. His simple instrument which was to revolutionize personal communication had only 12 parts. The Bell telephone which Western Electric makes today — simple, though it appears—has nearly 500 parts.

But if the telephone’s parts have multiplied by forty, its convenience and usefulness have multiplied, too, until we talk clearly and dependably across the country and across the seas. Today, some telephones have controls which raise the volume of sound for better hearing. Some have push-buttons for switching calls. Some have a light over the dial, for dark places. Some let you talk without lifting the handset. And there are instruments which answer when you’re away and record messages.

These modern conveniences are manufactured by Western Electric people, carrying on work begun for the Bell System back in 1882. We’ve had a big role in telephone progress over the years. Our main job is to translate laboratory ideas into efficient, economical quantity production.

Behind the scenes in Bell central offices you can see our handiwork. Where calls once were switched entirely by hand, there are now rows of automatic switching equipment which respond when your finger twirls the dial. And even faster, better switching equipment is on the way.

Today, equipment made by Western Electric permits a single pair of copper pathways to carry up to 1,800 conversations at once. And the towers of radio relay transmission systems march across the country to help speed calls and TV programs over distances.

Where can the pursuit of better telephone communications lead?

One intriguing forecast is this: Some day everybody will be given a telephone number at birth and, equipped with a push-button watch-sized instrument, will be able to call and to see anyone, anywhere.

Incredible? No more so than 1956 telephone service would have appeared to people 80 years ago. But whatever shape the future of telephone service may take, Western Electric—fulfilling its function as Bell System manufacturer—will engineer machines and processes for making what is needed... turning ideas into products skillfully and economically so Bell telephone service may be even more useful and convenient.
