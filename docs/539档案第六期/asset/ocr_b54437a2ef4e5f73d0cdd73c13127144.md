# 52、营养有多重？

![](bbox=[0.225, 0.192, 0.755, 0.354])

There are still some folks in the "fried potatoes for breakfast" school who are inclined to measure nourishment by the weight of a meal.

But fortunately for the stylists of everything from cars to clothing (as well as for our general health), new discoveries in nutrition are gradually correcting such opinions.

Most of these discoveries are coming out of laboratories, and are gradually finding their way to American tables. But one of them is coming right out of the breakfast habits of this new trim generation that likes everything light.

These folks are discovering Kellogg's Rice Krispies as the cereal of nutrition without weight. The one that's refreshing to the morning appetite. Light on the spoon, the tongue and the tummy. But high in food value— because it delivers important nourishment of whole grain rice.

Rice, you may recall, has been one of man's chief fueling grains back since the days when he had to stoke up to lick his

weight in wildcats. And its modern cereal form—Kellogg's Rice Krispies—still supplies important whole grain values.

Surprisingly enough, as well known and popular as this cereal is, it has long been waiting for the adult world to grow young and modern enough to appreciate a morning food that's light but nourishing. Kids know it as the world's only talking cereal—the one that says "Snap! Crackle! Pop!" when you pour on milk or cream.

But don't let that light-hearted manner fool you. Rice Krispies will stay right with you—give you vitamins, minerals, and energy generators today's pace calls for.

Of course, you won't out-super Superman, or even run faster for the bus just because you've breakfasted on a bowlful of Kellogg's Rice Krispies. But you will realize you've eaten well. And you'll know you've given your body a food that's light in everything but nutrition. Isn't that the way you like to start your mornings?
