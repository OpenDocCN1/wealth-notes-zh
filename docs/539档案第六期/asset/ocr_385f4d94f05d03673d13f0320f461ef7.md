# How the “City of Youth” helps Sears
develop an almost kid-proof shoe

Sears, Roebuck and Co. tests its products on the most modern equipment money can buy. But for testing children’s shoes, Sears adds one element: active children. Read how they helped perfect a remarkable new shoe.

The Sears laboratory has over 600 research assistants at Maryville Academy in Des Plaines, Illinois. Sears asks the children at this “City of Youth” to try on, wear and wear out a thousand pairs of shoes a year. What does Sears learn from this testing? How to make better children’s shoes. Take the X-15 boys’ shoe for example. The boys at Maryville wore the X-15. They scraped the soles. Scuffed the uppers. Crushed the backs. But the X-15 lasted longer than any other shoe Sears knows about. The Sears laboratory was so impressed they approved the X-15 as a Sears Four Star value item.

Today you can buy the X-15 at Sears in five styles—all under $7. Every pair carries this guarantee: The soles and heels are guaranteed to outwear the uppers or return the shoes for a new pair free of charge. The X-15 shoe is one of over 20,000 individual items tested in the Sears laboratory last year. This laboratory has absolute veto power over any product that does not measure up to its standards. No item it rejects can ever be sold at Sears. Shop at Sears and save. You’ll be surprised at the values you’ll find. They are there, thanks to the constant searching of Sears scientists. And helpers like the children at Maryville.

The children of Maryville test Sears shoes all year ‘round. The long-wearing X-15 they tested can be found at Sears stores and in the Sears catalog. Remember, you can charge it at Sears.
