By Jay C. Harris

Do you know only about 2 out of 10 American housewives follow —or even read—package directions? If you find that hard to believe, think back: … did you pour or measure the detergent the last time you used your automatic washer?

It makes a difference. For example, if you were the mother of five and followed directions on the back of an all package as carefully as you do with a cake mix, you could save enough in a year to pay for the gasoline used on a 300-mile vacation trip!

Startling … but true! Because all lets you measure clean, white washes by the spoonful! all is the condensed, super-rinse "controlled suds" detergent—specially developed for best results from automatic washers.

And the same creative chemistry that produced "controlled suds" for automatics now introduces AE-160, the answer to washday's biggest complaint: Whites don't wash white enough.

The new whitening discovery, AE-160, proved its effectiveness in Monsanto's Dayton laboratories. Here, cottons, linens, even nylons—yellowed by age or suds scum—washed back to pure white again without bleaching! Where whites had deep-set stains or dye transfers, the use of a household bleach helped the whitening action of new all.

You can now wash your yellowed cottons, linens, nylons, even washable heirloom fabrics back to lovely white, gently, without bleaching—thanks to new all with AE-160.

The first "controlled suds" detergent, all brought measured economy to an America accustomed to measuring washing power by billows of suds. With the development of automatic washers, manufacturers realized the need for a detergent without thick suds, one that could wash cleaner, rinse easier and take better care of the washer, too. Monsanto created the answer: all. Now the millions of automatic washer owners who have used all know that clothes wash cleaner in "controlled suds" because more dirt rinses out.
