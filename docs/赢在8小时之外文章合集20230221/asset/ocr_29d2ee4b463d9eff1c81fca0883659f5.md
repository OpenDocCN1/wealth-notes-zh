# How "overcompensation" can backfire

![](bbox=[0.32, 0.12, 0.68, 0.23])

Suppose a total stranger bumps into you carelessly and knocks your tablet computer to the ground, causing £100 damage, but offers to provide you £1,000 for your loss and troubles. A nice gesture, one might say, something that could build the victim’s admiration and trust for the perpetrator.

But research by a team including David De Cremer, a professor at Cambridge Judge Business School and the new KPMG chair in Management Studies, finds that such overcompensation usually backfires: compared to compensation equal to the loss, overcompensation offers no benefits in building trust and cooperation in the future, and “even provokes negative outcomes” such as suspicion and guilt – potentially damaging any future relationship between the two people.

举个例子：你在大街上玩平板电脑，结果来了一个愣头青把你的平板撞到地上了，摔坏了，想要修好平板需要花费1千元。但是呢，那个人却直接塞给你1万元。

这种“过度补偿”看似很友好，很大方，但是你得到1万元之后，内心会产生非常奇怪的变化，甚至会带来一系列负面的结果。

比如，你回到家之后，越想越觉得内疚：我本可以当场拒绝他，只拿走应有的赔偿金，其余的都还给他，结果我还是没有把持住，拿了这不义之财，哎，越想越后悔！

再比如，你可能会觉得对方是个蠢货：这个傻蛋，1万元我都能买两个新的平板电脑了，你是得多愚蠢才会这样浪费钱。再比如，你可能会怀疑对方是不是嚣张跋扈的富二代，撞坏别人的设备不但没有悔意，还拿钱侮辱人。
