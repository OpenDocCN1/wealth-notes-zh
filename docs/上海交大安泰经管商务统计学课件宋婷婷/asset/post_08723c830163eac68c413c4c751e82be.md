
## Example: Lot-Acceptance Sampling

- On the other hand, if \(\bar{x} > 116.71\), then \(H_0\) cannot be rejected and the shipment will be accepted.
- Assume \(\mu = 112\) (non-qualified shipment). What is the probability of accepting the shipment (type II error)?

\[
\beta = P(\bar{x} > 116.71 \mid \mu = 112) = P\left(z = \frac{\bar{x}-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} > 2.36\right) = \bar{\Phi}(2.36) = 0.91\%
\]

- If the population mean is 112 hours, the probability of making a type II error is 0.91%.

## Control Type II Error（备注：这里有发现丁丁）

- In general, for lower-tailed \(Z\)-test procedure, its Type II error probability is

\[
\beta = P(\text{Type II error}) = P\left( \frac{\bar{X}-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} > -z_\alpha \mid H_a \right) = \Phi\left( z_\alpha - \frac{\sqrt{n}(\mu_0-\mu_a)}{\sigma} \right).
\]

- Determine \(n\), the number of samples required, to ensure the Type II error probability is no larger than \(\beta\),

\[
z_\alpha - \frac{\sqrt{n}(\mu_0-\mu_a)}{\sigma} = -z_\beta
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
n = \frac{(z_\alpha + z_\beta)^2 \sigma^2}{(\mu_0-\mu_a)^2}.
\]

## Summary: One Sample Hypothesis Testing

1. State the \(H_0\) and \(H_a\).
   - Statement about population parameter: \(H_0: \mu = \mu_0;\ H_a: \mu \neq \mu_0\). \(H_0: \mu \geq \mu_0;\ H_a: \mu < \mu_0\). \(H_0: \mu \leq \mu_0;\ H_a: \mu > \mu_0\)
   - Example: quality control / drug effectiveness / proportion

2. Select the level of significance \(\alpha\).
   - Control type I error: 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.1

3. Calculate test statistics based on sample data.
   - Z-test (with known variance): \(z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}\) or \(z = \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}}\)
   - t-test (with unknown variance): \(t = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{S/\sqrt{n}}\)

4. Make a decision rule based on probability and statistic theory.
   - Know the probability distribution behind test statistics.
   - Critical value or p-value approach.

5. Collect a random sample and draw a conclusion.
   - Understand the risk behind the conclusion: Type I error or Type II error.

## Testing the Difference between Two Population Means

- **One-sample hypothesis testing when**
  - the variance is known \(\Rightarrow\) Z-test
  - the variance is unknown \(\Rightarrow\) T-test
- **Two-sample hypothesis testing when**
  - the samples are independent, their variances are known
  - the samples are independent, their variances are equal but unknown
  - the samples are independent, their variances are unequal and unknown
  - the samples are matched and correlated

## Inference about Two Means: Motivating Example

- A grocery retailer operates two stores. One is in the inner city and the other is in a suburban shopping center.
- The manager notices that the products that sell well in one store may not sell well in the other.
- This situation may be attributable to differences in customer demographics at the two locations.
- The manager asks us to investigate the difference between the mean ages of the customers who shop at the two stores.

## Sampling Distribution for the Difference between Two Population Means

- Assume we randomly select \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) customers from the inner-city and suburban stores, respectively,
  - \(X_{11}, X_{12}, \ldots, X_{1n_1} \sim N(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2)\),
  - \(X_{21}, X_{22}, \ldots, X_{2n_2} \sim N(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2)\),
  - The two samples are independent.
- The point estimator for \(\mu_1 - \mu_2\) is \(\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2\). What is the sampling distribution?
- **Key point:** the sum of two independent normal random variables is still normal, with its mean being the sum of two means, and its variance being the sum of two variances.

## Interval Estimation for \(\mu_1 - \mu_2\)

- Since

\[
\bar{x}_1 \sim N\left(\mu_1, \frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}\right), \quad \bar{x}_2 \sim N\left(\mu_2, \frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}\right)
\]

we have

\[
\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2 \sim N\left(\mu_1 - \mu_2, \frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}\right)
\]

- The **standard error** for the estimator is \(\sigma_{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2/n_1 + \sigma_2^2/n_2}\).

- When both \(\sigma_1^2\) and \(\sigma_2^2\) are known, we have **interval estimation** for \(\mu_1 - \mu_2\),

\[
\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2 \pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}
\]

with confidence coefficient of \(1 - \alpha\).
