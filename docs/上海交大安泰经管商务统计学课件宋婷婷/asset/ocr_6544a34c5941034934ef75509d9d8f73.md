# Two Population Means when Variances are Known

Consider the two-tailed hypothesis test

+   Step 1: State the hypotheses
+   
+   $H_0: \mu_1 - \mu_2 = D_0$    versus    $H_a: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ne D_0$.

+   Step 3: **We need a test statistic whose distribution is known under $H_0$.**

+   When $\sigma_1^2, \sigma_2^2$ are known, the test statistic is specified by
+   
+   $$z = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2 - D_0}{\sqrt{\sigma_1^2/n_1 + \sigma_2^2/n_2}}.$$

+   Step 4: Under $H_0$, we have $z \sim N(0,1)$. Therefore, the decision rule of Z-test will be used.
