# Two Population Means when $\sigma_1^2 \ne \sigma_2^2$ are Unknown

+   - Next assume the population variances $\sigma_1^2 \ne \sigma_2^2$ are unknown.
+   - Step 1: Consider the two-tailed hypothesis test
+     $H_0: \mu_1 - \mu_2 = D_0 \quad \text{versus} \quad H_a: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ne D_0$.
+   - Step 3: The $t$-test should be used and the corresponding $t$-test statistic is
+     $$t = \frac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2-D_0}{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}}.$$
+   - Even under $H_0$, the distribution of $t$ is **not known**. Why?
+   - A student $t$-distribution approximation for $t$ statistic is constructed as
+     $$t \sim t_{df}, \quad \text{under } H_0,$$
+     where
+     $$df = \frac{(s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2)^2}{\frac{(s_1^2/n_1)^2}{n_1-1}+\frac{(s_2^2/n_2)^2}{n_2-1}}.$$
