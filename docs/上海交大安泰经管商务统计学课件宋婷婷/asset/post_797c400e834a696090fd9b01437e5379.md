
## Berkson's Paradox

### Example 1: University Applications in U.S.

- Two common requirements: **high school GPA** and **SAT** scores.
- **Question:** What is the relationship between **high school GPA** and **SAT** for students?
- Your guess: the high school GPA and SAT scores are **positively correlated**.
- **Question:** What is the relationship between **high school GPA** and **SAT** for students who attend a **middle-rank college**?
- What is your guess now?
- **Middle-rank college requirements:** Either a sufficiently high GPA or a sufficiently high SAT scores.

![](img/ea9cdfad8dc7821ead4e68fc7aaa5ca3_29_0.png)

- **Excellent Group**: Who have both high GPAs and high SAT scores will likely get into a higher-tier school.
- **Middle Group**: The range of students that actually attend the school is given by the blue dots in the plot.
- **Results:**
  - A downward trend in the middle-rank college
  - An upward trend for the overall population
- A selection bias!

### Example 2: Dating Problem

- Interesting Facts: Among the people you date (or you may date in future), the attractive ones tend to be jerks?
  - Psycho-social theories
  - A simple statistic explanation: Selection bias!
    - Your choice of people to date depends on two factors: attractiveness and personality.

| Attractiveness | Nice (暖) | Mean (渣) |
|---|---|---|
| Attractive (美) | ✓ | ✓ |
| Unattractive (丑) | ✓ | × |

Results: When you censored the mean unattractive one, this creates a spurious negative correlation between attractiveness and personality.

## Simpson's Paradox

### Example: Gender Bias

- Some Facts: The enrollment rate for female and male in a university

| Gender | Applicant | Recruit | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Female | 120 | 50 | 42% |
| Male | 120 | 25 | 21% |

- Boys in that university try to protest over the low male enrollment rate.

- Do you think the university has sexual discrimination?

- A Detailed Fact:

| School | Female Applicant | Female Recruit | Female Rate | Male Applicant | Male Recruit | Male Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Business School | 100 | 49 | 49% | 20 | 15 | 75% |
| Law School | 20 | 1 | 5% | 100 | 10 | 10% |

- Conclusion: There is a trend in several different sets of data, but when these data sets are combined, this trend disappears or reverses.

- Confounding variable: Because School is a hidden variable that cannot be ignored.

## Tabular Displays

Categorical variable

- Categorical data: Data that can be grouped by specific categories
- Tabular display methods:
  - Frequency Distribution (频数表)
  - Relative Frequency Distribution (相对频数分布)
  - Percent Frequency Distribution (百分数频数表)
