
## Example

- Upper-tail Hypotheses:

  \(H_0: \sigma^2 \le 4\)

  \(H_a: \sigma^2 > 4\)

- Test statistics:

  \[
  \chi^2 = \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma^2} = \frac{23 \times 4.9}{4} = 28.18
  \]

- Rejection Rule: Reject \(H_0\) if \(\chi^2 \ge \chi^2_\alpha\) (or if p-value \(\le \alpha\))
- In this example, \(\chi^2_{.10}(23)=32.007\) (p-value = 0.2091).
- Conclusion: We cannot reject \(H_0\).

## Inferences About Two Population Variances: Example

- Statistically: 31 men and 41 women are selected to take a survey to study their attitudes on LoL global finals.

- Random Sample: The results provide a sample variance of \(s_1^2 = 120\) for women and a sample variance of \(s_2^2 = 80\) for men.

- Statistical Model: The hypothesis:

  \(H_0: \sigma_1^2 \le \sigma_2^2\)

  \(H_a: \sigma_1^2 > \sigma_2^2\)

## Inferences About Two Population Variances

- **Questions:** We are interested to compare the variances from two different populations.
- **Sample data:** We use data collected from two independent random samples, one from population 1 and another from population 2.
- The two sample variances will be the basis for making inferences about the two population variances.
- **Some other examples:**
  - compare the variances in product quality resulting from two different production processes;
  - compare the variances in temperatures for two heating devices;
  - compare the variances in assembly times for two assembly methods.
- How to construct test statistics? Sampling distribution?

## F Distribution

- The *F* distribution with \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) degrees of freedom is the distribution of

  \[
  \frac{U_1/d_1}{U_2/d_2},
  \]

  where \(U_1 \sim \chi^2(d_1)\), \(U_2 \sim \chi^2(d_2)\), \(U_1\) and \(U_2\) are **independent**.

- Assume simple random samples and normal populations. When \(\sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2\), the sampling distribution of

  \[
  \frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2}
  \]

  is an *F* distribution with \(n_1 - 1\) and \(n_2 - 1\) degrees of freedom. *(Why?)*

## Inferences About Two Population Variances

- Step 1: state the null and alternative hypotheses

  \(H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2\); \(H_a: \sigma_1^2 \neq \sigma_2^2\).

- Step 2: select the level of significance \(\alpha\).

- Step 3: the test statistic is specified by

  \[
  F = \frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2} \sim F(n_1 - 1, n_2 - 1),
  \]

  where \(s_1^2\) is the sample variance of population 1, \(s_2^2\) is the sample variance of population 2.

- Step 4: Two approaches to determine the rejection rule
  - Critical-value approach
  - p-value approach
