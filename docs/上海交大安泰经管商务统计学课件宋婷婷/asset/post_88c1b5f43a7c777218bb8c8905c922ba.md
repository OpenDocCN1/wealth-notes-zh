
## Type I Error Rates for Multiple Comparisons

- One approach for controlling α_EW is the Bonferroni adjustment—the overall α_EW=0.05.
- How?
- We simply adjust the comparisonwise error rate α = α_EW / C_k².

| k | C_k² | α_EW (α=0.05) | α_EW (α=0.05 / C_k²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3 | 0.1426 | 0.0492 |
| 5 | 10 | 0.4013 | 0.0489 |
| 10 | 45 | 0.9006 | 0.0488 |
| 20 | 190 | 0.9999 | 0.0488 |

- **Criticism:** The correction comes at the cost of increasing the probability of producing false negatives, i.e., increasing type 2 error β.

## A Brief Introduction to DOE (实验设计)

- An **experiment** is a controlled study in which the researcher attempts to understand **cause-and-effect relationships** (因果关系).
- **Design of experiments** (DOE) is a branch of statistics on planning, conducting and analyzing the experiments.
- DOE is founded by Sir Ronald A. Fisher. Fisher applied DOE to the field of agriculture as early as the 1920s, when he was the primary statistician at the Rothamsted Experimental Station.
- Now it is the fundamental method for comparing the effects of different treatments in virtually all fields of research.

## The Influence of Randomized Controlled Trials on Development Economics Research and on Development Policy

Paper prepared for "The State of Economics, The State of the World" Conference proceedings volume

Abhijit Vinayak Banerjee Esther Duflo Michael Kremer September 11, 2014

Many (though far from all) of the questions that development economists and policymakers ask themselves are causal in nature. What would be the impact of adding computers in classrooms? What is the price elasticity of demand for preventative health products? Would increasing interest rates lead to an increase in savings? In the past, the standard method to answer such causal questions was Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs). Fisher, 1925. In an RCT, the assignment of different units to different treatments is done at random. This randomization has an important characteristic: the resulting differences in outcomes can be attributed to the treatment, and hence any difference between treatment and control units reflects the impact of the treatment. While this idea is simple, the implementation in the field can be more involved, and it took some time before randomization was considered to be a practical tool for answering questions in social science, research in general, and in development economics more specifically.

## Steps for Designing an Experiment

The practical steps needed for designing an experiment include:

- 1 Recognition and statement of the problem
- 2 Choice of factors, levels, and ranges
- 3 Selection of the response variable(s)
- 4 Choice of design
- 5 Conducting the experiment
- 6 Statistical analysis (统计分析)
- 7 Drawing conclusions, and making recommendations

In this lecture, we shall focus on choice of the design (completely randomized design/randomized block design/factorial design) and statistical analysis (ANOVA).

## Completely Randomized Design (完全随机设计)

- A completely randomized design (CRD) is an experimental design for studying the effects of one factor, in which the treatments are randomly assigned to the experimental units.

- In the example of AutoShine, Inc., in order to study if three different waxes are equally effective in durability, we adopt the completely randomized design.

- Randomly pick 5 new cars and wax them with Type 1, 5 with Type 2, and 5 with Type 3.

- Each car was then repeatedly run through an automatic carwash until the wax coating showed signs of deterioration, and the number of washes is recorded.

- We can carry out the analysis for CRD using one-way ANOVA.

## Randomized Block Design (随机区组化设计)

- When single experimental units are sampled among highly **heterogeneous conditions** (异质性), it is unlikely that these single units will adequately represent the populations and repeated sampling is likely to yield very different outcomes.
- **Blocking** can be used to form homogeneous groups, resulting in a Randomized Block Design.
