
## Example

- A company's personnel director wants to determine if there are differences in the performance ratings among the managers who graduated from three colleges
- The hypotheses are
  - H₀ : All populations are identical in terms of performance
  - Hₐ : Not all populations are identical in terms of performance
- We will use a 0.05 level of significance for the test.
- Independent samples of performance rating data are available

## The Procedure

- Rank the combined data from the lowest to highest values; the tied values are assigned the average rank
- Sum the ranks for each sample
- We use the sum of the ranks to construct the test statistic

| College A | Rank | College B | Rank | College C | Rank |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25 | 3 | 6 | 1 | 5 | 1 |
| 70 | 12 | 20 | 2 | 70 | 12 |
| 60 | 9 | 30 | 4 | 76 | 13 |
| 85 | 17 | 45 | 6 | 80 | 14.5 |
| 95 | 20 | 40 | 5 | 90 | 18.5 |
| 90 | 18.5 | 35 | 3 | 70 | 12 |
| 80 | 14.5 | 50 | 7 | 75 | 14 |
| **Sum of Ranks** | **95** | **Sum of Ranks** | **27** | **Sum of Ranks** | **88** |

## Sampling Distribution

- Kruskal-Wallis test statistic

$$H = \left[\frac{12}{n_T(n_T + 1)} \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{R_i^2}{n_i}\right] - 3(n_T + 1)$$

where $k$ is the number of populations, $n_i$ is the number of observations in sample $i$, $n_T$ is the total numbers of all the samples, and $R_i$ is the sum of the ranks for sample $i$

- $H$ resembles the computing formula for SSTr in the ANOVA

- If $H_0$ is true, the $H$ has approximately a $\chi^2_{k-1}$ distribution

- In our example, $n_1 = 7$, $n_2 = 6$, $n_3 = 7$, and $n_T = 20$. Therefore we can calculate $H = 8.92$, and the p-value

p-value $= P(\chi^2_2 \ge 8.92) = 0.0116 < \alpha$

and we reject $H_0$.
