# 4、客户名单中，隐藏着额外 40%的利润

There's probably an extra 40% more profit just sitting dormant in your past customer names. Here's an easy and practical way to gain access to all that money.

Dear Software Publisher:

Whether you normally sell by mail or through retail distribution your past customer list can be a rich source of enormous profit opportunities in the next six weeks.

There are four different ways to make hundreds of thousands of dollars from your list of previous software buyers. Let's review all four:

+   1. Prepare special mailings to segmented portions of your list offering them your other compatible software applications. In case you are unfamiliar with the profit dynamics of direct mail to one's own customer list, allow me to go through the rudiments with you.

It costs you about $300 to mail every thousand customer names. If we structure the mailing piece properly, we should produce a 2-5% response rate at an average order of $125. That translates to 20 to 50 people ordering at least one more program from you for every 1,000 customers--and possibly many more. At just 20 orders per 1,000 names mailed, you gross $2,500 from a $300 investment. If you have a 25,000 name customer list or larger, we can probably mail it four to ten times a year and produce in the vicinity of 2,000 to 5,000 additional $125 orders a year for you--all prepaid. That translates to $250,000-$625,000 in added gross sales for a maximum marketing cost of, perhaps, $30,000 or so.

Not a bad way to add dramatically to your bottom line or to shoot adrenaline into your cash flow.

But there are three additional ways to make money using that same list. Here they are.

+   2. Develop joint ventures with sellers of synergistic products and software whose products are perfectly suited to your customer list. If you don't have sufficient products to sell yourself, we can arrange joint ventures where all you put up is the customer names and the marketing piece (we'd write the sales letter). The other partner would put up the products and all the mailing costs. All orders would come back to you and you'd extract 25-30% of the gross sales as your fee. Of course, you can do it another way and make more of the profit by merely buying the products on a dropped ship basis from the other firm, if you don't mind putting up the modest mailing expenses.
