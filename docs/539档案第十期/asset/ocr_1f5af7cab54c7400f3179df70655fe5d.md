# 3、我想和你分享一个有趣的小故事......

I want to share with you a curious little story...

Dear Valued Customers:

About eight years ago—almost to the day—I first met Jim Cook, President of Investment Rarities Incorporated.

The company was tiny then—a dozen employees at most. (Today, they employ over 250.)

But I was impressed (even back then) with the commitment to service and the genuine desire Jim Cook instilled in his staff to sell IRI customers only investments that he genuinely believed they would make money on.

Keep in mind this was at a time when inflation was running rampant and profiteers and scoundrels were, quite literally, "weeping" out of the woodwork.

 Racketeers were pushing silver on margin, leverage, delayed delivery, you name it. But Jim Cook steadfastly refused to allow IRI to sell anything but a handful of super-conservative rare U.S. gold and silver coins—and, of course, gold and silver bullion—all for delivery into the customers' hands.

I have, in my files, a very homey letter Jim Cook earnestly sent to his customers and a few thousand prospects back in 1977, pleading with them to pick up at least a few rolls of Morgan silver dollars—and some St. Gaudens or Liberty $20 gold pieces—this back when rare coins were an almost unknown investment form.

Over the years I've watched, and in some modest way (I hope) contributed to the growth and success of Investment Rarities. I have been paid handsomely for guiding the marketing at IRI and I enjoy the relationship.

Today, I am very proud to say, IRI is unquestionably the largest, most ethical and principled investment coin dealer in the country.

Amazingly, they currently serve an excess of 100,000 satisfied repeat customers—I’m referring to investors who’ve dealt with IRI for as much as 10 years or longer. People who saw a $1,000 investment in rare coins grow to $10,000—and people who’ve received honest counsel and straightforward recommendations from Jim Cook’s company.

Everything in life isn’t always roses. And 1981 took its toll on IRI, along with everyone else.

And yet... I’ve seen Investment Rarities avoid the chance to make an almost instant $3-5 million—at a time when they really could have used the money—but Jim Cook didn’t feel the investment that they could have made all the money with was good for his clientele! So, he decided not to endorse or promote it—and in doing so, he lost out on a personal profit of over $3 million.
