Jay is very low profile. I personally think he's a charming, little curly haired eccentric. But he doesn't even try to promote himself. If he did, I'm quite certain he'd make over a million dollars a year.

But I'd better get to the point.

I tried to persuade him to contact magazines like Corporate Report. I felt he could write wonderfully valuable marketing articles and columns for you. But Jay's really shy. Instead of writing to you direct he spent many thousands of dollars on a P.R. agency - that has, so far, done him nothing.

Jay wrote a sample article for the P.R. people to try and place. They didn't even recognize it's importance.

I've included a copy of that original article - unedited for you to read - plus another delightfully enriching article Jay wrote three years ago. It's not about marketing - but the subject is probably more timely in today's high interest economy than it was three years ago - when he originally wrote it.

Frankly, Mr. Mundale, the reason I'm writing you is to prove to my boss that a magazine such as Corporate Report would love to write articles about him -- or at least reprint his articles for your readers.

Jay doesn't think magazines would be interested in his marketing perspective.

Mr. Mundale, how could you not be interested in the commentary of a man that singularly accounted for 965 million dollars in sales in 1980 alone?!

I personally think his P.R. agency is not serving him - or the magazines. That is why I am writing you this letter.

I ask you to read both of the articles I've included. I assure you - you'll be fascinated by their contents. Then - after reading them - if you wish to reprint either article - please do. Or if you want Jay to write a special article for you write me and I promise you he'll do it.
