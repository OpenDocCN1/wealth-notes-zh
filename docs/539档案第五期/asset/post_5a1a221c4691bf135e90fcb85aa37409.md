# in All Hospitals?

Condensed from Your Life
William S. Dutton

In a ward of the vast Philadelphia General Hospital reserved for policemen and firemen, a badly wounded officer had lain in coma for two weeks. Medical skill had been unable to bring him back to consciousness. His bed was screened off.
"You won’t disturb him," a nurse assured two Gray Ladies—volunteer Red Cross workers—a pianist and a singer. She added, "We’re afraid that nothing will."
A spinet piano was wheeled in, and several old melodies were softly played and sung. Then the singer began "When Irish Eyes Are Smiling." She had sung only a line when

a man’s voice joined hers—and there was no mistaking from what bed it came. The Gray Ladies did not falter, and the voice continued all the way through the song.
Afterward the head nurse sought them out. "You wonders, you!" she said. "More than Irish eyes are smiling! Our man is going to live!"
He had crossed the bridge offered by the Gray Ladies—a bridge as intangible as a rainbow, yet no less real to many in dire need.

在费城总医院留给警察和消防员的一间病房里，一个伤势非常重的人，已经昏迷躺了 2 个星期。医学方法一直无法让他恢复意识。他的床被屏风隔着。

“你们不要打扰他，”一个护士对两个灰衣女士——红十字会志愿工作者——一个钢琴家和一个歌手，说道。她又补充道，“不过，恐怕也没什么能打扰到他。”

灰衣女士推进来一架小型立式钢琴，开始演奏，一些旧旋律轻轻飘扬。然后，歌手轻唱“当爱尔兰人的眼睛微笑起来。”她唱完一句后，一个男人的声音飘了出来——毫无疑问，声音是从被隔开的那张床上飘过来的。灰衣女士没有停下来，继续轻唱。

他穿过了灰衣女士搭的桥——像彩虹一样无形的桥，然而，却和许多迫切需求的东西一样真实。
