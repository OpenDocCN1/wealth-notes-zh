# Failure Becomes Triumph

The new drug was one of many the Swiss researchers developed. Like the others, it proved useless as a pain killer. But one alert medical scientist noticed it had another property—it could calm the nerve center that controls coughing. Coughs may appear to originate in the throat or chest, but actually it is this Cough Control Center in the brain that "orders" the cough. Before the Swiss discovery, only narcotics such as codeine could quiet this Center. But codeine can cause stomach upsets, cramps, dizziness: and can be habit-forming. The new discovery, not a narcotic, suppressed nagging coughs without these sickening side-effects.
