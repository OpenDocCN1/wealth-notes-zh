Do you remember specifically when members of your family had chest X-rays?
Do you know exactly when your young child had his last tetanus "shot"?
Do you recall the exact dates of your last stay in the hospital?
You can never tell when answers to such questions may be needed. And the only way to be sure of having information of this sort readily available is to keep, with the help of your doctor, an accurate, up-to-date health record. It can be one of your most important, if unofficial, documents.
Suppose, for example, that you moved

and consulted a new physician. Facts from your own health record might alert him to certain conditions he should know about.
A health record can also be of great help when entering a hospital, or when applying for a new job or when you are traveling. And, in a medical emergency, a health record can be time-saving—perhaps lifesaving.
If you've never kept a personal health record, Metropolitan Life now makes it easy. There's a four-page form for this purpose in the booklet, Your Guide to Good Health.
To get your free copy, simply call your local Metropolitan office or mail the coupon.
