On April 20, customers at Serena Hotel and Marriott Hotel in Islamabad suddenly received a call from the reception desk. To welcome the upcoming US-Iran talks, all customers were asked to check out in advance.

At the same time, flight information showed that four flights carrying US negotiation personnel had already landed in Pakistan and entered through the VIP channel.

However, at this last moment, Iran announced it would no longer participate in negotiations.

I believe we have all seen too many similar messages these past two days. Iran says it will participate in negotiations one moment, and then doesn't, what's going on?

The root lies in the different interest groups within Iran—the moderate civilian official group and the hardline Revolutionary Guard. Their attitudes have diverged.

## 01 Why does Iran's civilian government want to negotiate?

Since the war began at the end of February, Iranian society has faced serious shocks. However, strangely enough, the initial impact of the war was not large. But after the ceasefire began, prices in Iran started to skyrocket. Among them fruits such as oranges increased 55%, pears increased 68%, while vegetables generally increased more than 90%. A worker's monthly wage, after deducting necessary expenses such as medical fees, rent, bank loans, water and electricity bills, etc., could only buy a 10 kg bag of rice, 5 kg of red meat, 5 pieces of chicken or fish, 2 liters of cooking oil and a few kilograms of fruit.

Even more exaggeratedly, the current Iran saw umbrella prices double, even with installment payments.

This is only the first layer of the economic crisis.

Because the war caused inflation, the Iranian government required enterprises to increase the minimum wage by 60% in April to ensure residents could support themselves, then the chain reaction came. For enterprises relying on imported raw materials, the collapse of the Iranian exchange rate made it difficult for them to produce, and after labor costs further increased, local enterprises saw no hope left.

According to a high-level person in a construction company, their parent company laid off at least 1000 people, and more seriously, he contacted 11 suppliers, all of which have already gone bankrupt. Because the suppliers' profits were no longer enough to pay the rising wages.

It can be said that for the pro-peace faction in Iran, negotiating with the United States is not bowing the head, but a helplessness move of the current Iranian situation.

So Iranian representatives have repeatedly released good will, and even their Foreign Minister Araghi expressed on Twitter that the Strait of Hormuz can be opened.

## 02 To some extent, today's US blockade, and ship detention is directly targeting Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.

Here is a key piece of information: although the Strait of Hormuz was once blockaded, Iran's own oil exports were not truly throttled. On the contrary, under the control and operation of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, oil exports for the foreign sector once hit a high point of 2.2 million barrels/day during the war period in March, with an average export volume around 1.8 million barrels/day, while this number before the war was roughly only 1.5 to 1.6 million barrels/day.

This means that the war did not make certain interest groups bleed, but instead made them earn more.

At the same time, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps can also charge about $20 million per day in "tolls" from the Strait of Hormuz. In other words, in the eyes of the outside world, it was a battlefield of gunfire, but in the eyes of some people it was actual cash flow.

Before the US blockade, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps had a very comfortable life. Now, with the naval blockade of US military ships, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' fiscal income has been cut off. This will also further promote Iran's internal inflation level.

Although in the current environment, Iran can still solve a part of the material supply problem through land transportation, land transportation is far behind sea transportation in terms of transportation scale and cost.

That is to say, once the United States continues to tighten the blockade in the direction of the Gulf of Oman, Iran's internal economic pressure will grow even bigger. In this way, the negotiation balance will naturally tilt towards the United States side again.

Time drags on longer, the social and economic costs borne by Iran domestically will become heavier and heavier.

This is why Iran's Parliament Speaker Kharabieh publicly accused Trump, claiming that the US side implemented the blockade, trying to turn the "negotiation table" into a "surrender table".
Because this round of US operations hit Iran's lifeline.

## 03 Actually, the White House's operation conforms to strategic logic, as long as the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps can rely on the Strait of Hormuz to profit, Iranian representatives will find it difficult to concede on this issue.

If the United States hopes that the final agreement conforms to its own interests, it must cut off this interest chain.

The most decisive move in blocking the strait lies in avoiding the possibility of Iran's fish dying and net breaking, and moving the main battlefield from around Iran to the Strait of Oman.

It should be noted that all of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' war preparations are designed based on US military attacks on their home country. Once their homeland is attacked, Iran can launch attacks on surrounding countries through drones and missiles, thereby destroying the oil production capabilities of these producing countries, forcing the West to give up through high oil prices.

However, when the US military's strategy changes from killing Iran to starving Iran, Iran loses the reason to attack surrounding oil-producing countries.

For Iran, as long as crude oil cannot be transported out, this effect is the same as destroying oil facilities. Similarly, when the battlefield shifts from around Iran to the **Gulf of Oman**, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' weaknesses in lacking navy and air force immediately came to the forefront.

For the hardliners, Iran has now lost an important bargaining chip.

It can only be said that the US blockade of the Gulf of Oman is indeed a brilliant move, but the problem is that neither the pro-peace faction nor the hardliners in Iran respond. No matter how well you play the chess, I just don't want to play, this makes it difficult for the United States.

On the 20th, Iran's Supreme Leader Hamenei reiterated three basic positions, namely war compensation, management of the Strait of Hormuz, and treating the resistance front as a whole, and through this method re-strengthened the negotiation position.

In short, the current situation, compared to the first negotiation, has taken a big step backward.

To see this, many friends are probably asking: What should Iran do next? Can't just keep not negotiating, not fighting, and not settling, just stiffening?

I think, the road in front of Iran actually has only one left — drag.

Currently, Iran's public attitude is clear: If the United States does not lift the blockade, it will never negotiate.

Simply put, this itself is also a 施压 means, wanting to force the United States to make concessions first. But the problem is, when Trump knows that blockade is useful, he will never release first.

So, the most possible and realistic option right now is actually that Iran and the United States simultaneously open the Strait of Hormuz, both sides restore navigation, and then restart negotiations on this basis. This has almost become the optimal solution for the current de-escalation situation.

Iran is now able to utilize an important time variable, which is the domestic political cycle of the United States.

The real time pressure of the Trump administration is before the midterm elections. Once the election stage is entered, an Iranian war that has yet to end will inevitably become the political ammunition repeatedly attacking the White House by the Democratic Party. The closer to the election, the more the Trump government hopes to deal with the Iran issue as soon as possible, and the more likely it is to concede in certain conditions.

But Iran may not wait for it to end.

Looking at Iran's internal economic situation, inflation, blockade, and supply chain damage are all tangible consumption. US political pressure may rise as the election approaches, but pressure within Iran will also increase with time.

If dragging, must also find a way to bypass at the same time. So-called "bypass" essentially means bypassing the current US sea blockade system, finding new routes. One is the Caspian Sea route from Russia; the other is the Russia-Azerbaijan-Iran railway. As long as these two lines can stably transport supplies to Iran, Iran still has a chance.

In terms of oil exports, must also try to utilize the smuggling network in the Balochistan direction of Pakistan as well as the third-party trans-shipment system to export crude oil.

- Simply put, Iran's first stage fluctuations are the pro-peace faction and the hardliners, basing their own interests debating on negotiation conditions: the moderate government civil officials attempted to end the war, while the hardline Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps needed to continue earning from the war contradiction.
- After the United States detained the ship, the contradiction began to change.
- Within the hardline faction, it began to divide into two factions, one is the hardline pragmatic faction, the representative figure is Iran Parliament Speaker Kharabieh, his attitude is similar to Foreign Minister Araghi, hoping to stop the war.
- Use the "open the Strait of Hormuz" chip to exchange for US chips, such as lifting the blockade, lifting sanctions, etc.
- The other, is the extreme hardline faction, they strongly oppose any concessions, must continue to blockade the strait, this cake eaten up absolutely will not spit out, until obtain Iran's desired things.
- Three factions have different attitudes, which is the source of Iran's attitude reversing back and forth.
- US-Iran both parties, have entered the endurance competition stage, who blinks first, who may lose this war.
