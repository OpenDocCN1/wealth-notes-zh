Look closely at the picture above and you’ll find some potentially dangerous situations that could cause accidents in your own home.

Notice the turned-up corner of the rug, the skates on the floor and the books on the staircase. These are invitations to fall—and falls cause over 13,000 home fatalities a year.

Always keep stairways free of obstructions, provide a storage place for toys—and teach children to keep them there. Rugs, of course, should be made trip and slide-proof.

See the open fire without a screen? It’s estimated that over 1,500 homes are attacked by fire each day. Every fireplace should be screened—especially for the safety of toddlers.

Did you spot the frayed electric cord leading to the table lamp? It could cause severe shock, or a painful burn. Be sure that all the cords and electrical outlets throughout your home are always in good repair.

Notice the heavy vase on the table? A youngster could reach it easily . . . and pull it over on himself. Keep heavy objects centered on tables.

What about other potential "danger spots" in your home? The bathroom can be especially dangerous—if medicines are left where young children can get at them. All medicines should be kept in a secure storage place. And so should bleach, lye, insecticides and cleaning fluids.

> Poisoning from such substances is now a common medical emergency among young children.

Last year home accidents took the lives of 28,000 Americans and caused over four million disabling injuries. Why not inspect your home for potential sources of accidents—and make necessary repairs or arrange for their removal?
