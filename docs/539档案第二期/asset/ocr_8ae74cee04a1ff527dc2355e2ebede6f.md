# 39、科学是如何打破厚厚的泡沫之谜的

Automatic washer manufacturers demanded a new kind of detergent. Chemistry responded with a formula that had everything—everything, that is, except old-fashioned thick suds.

THE REQUIREMENTS washer makers outlined for an ideal detergent were challenging. It must be able to clean at least as well or better than available detergents. It must be able to suspend dirt once it is removed, until the rinse cycle begins. It must not make thick suds. It has been found early by washer makers that thick suds were inad

quare for automatic washing. They slowed washing action, frequently refused to rinse: often overflowed to clog delicate washer parts, leading to expensive repairs.

In answer, chemistry created all. all with "controlled suds" has been busting the thick-suds bubble ever since. Instead of choking washing action with a smothering blanket of suds on top of the water, "controlled suds" wash underwater where the clothes are. Instead of clinging to clothes during rinsing, "controlled suds" drain away almost as easily as water itself.

Today, still in its infancy compared to competitive soaps and detergents, all ranks first in sales among detergents made and prescribed exclusively for automatic washers. Indeed, it is credited with a large part of the success of the automatic washer itself.

And housewives have proven that all is economical. all is a condensed detergent, measurable by the tablespoon. One tablespoon of all gives wash water greater washability than three tablespoons of the leading thick-suds products.

To match the cleaning concept of "controlled suds"—a remarkable new whitening discovery has recently been added to the all formula. Now linens, cottons, and nylons "yellowed" by age or suds scum deposits can be washed back to white again... gently, without bleaching.
