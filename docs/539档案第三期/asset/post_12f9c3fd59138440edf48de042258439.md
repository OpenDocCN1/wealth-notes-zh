## The prize catch
in comfort

A revealing insight on clothes by a man whose leisure hours lead him to the great outdoors: C. P. Smith, President of American Airlines

![](img/4a4110a48dd9dbd39e3d48b14a410300_203_0.png)

"I take my annual holiday on the Great Watshishu River in Quebec, Canada, fishing for Atlantic salmon. I have learned from long experience that wool clothing is needed to make it a pleasant holiday."

"Wool shirts or sweaters for the cool days. Wool underwear for the cold. Wool blankets for the night air. A wool greatcoat for an evening trip up the St. Lawrence. Wool pants to keep out the cold of icy water. Wool felt for the soles of the waders to avoid slipping on the rocks; wool socks to be worn inside them."

"Wool serves us well and makes life more pleasant and enjoyable. It would be difficult to do without the substantial benefits we derive from wool."

The reason Mr. Smith can count on wool's comfort and performance is that nature itself builds many unique features into the wool fiber.

For one thing, it provides every wool fiber with a waxy shield that repels rain and snow. And strangely enough, this shield also has tiny pores that absorb moisture vapor. That's why wool clothing protects you against the weather but never gives you that clammy, sealed-in feeling.

Nature also gives the wool fiber a crimp or "permanent wave." These crimped fibers form millions of tiny air pockets that provide insulation and keep you warm—protect you against temperature changes.

What's more, this same crimp gives the wool fiber unique elasticity. It can actually be stretched 30% and still return to shape. That's why wool gives you the freedom to swing a golf club or cast your line—without binding or restricting. And why wool clothing always looks so good, wears so well for so long.

Your own experience will tell you—for a quiet walk in the woods or a rugged camping trip—American-made wools give you unmatched comfort. Nothing measures up to wool.

## 53、如何在 90°的高温热浪中让自己感觉像春天般温暖
## How to keep warm in a 90° heat wave

Report on a paradox by Jim Bishop, syndicated columnist and author of "The Day Christ Died" and "The Day Lincoln was Shot"

![](img/4a4110a48dd9dbd39e3d48b14a410300_207_0.png)

Dallas in the summertime is hot. July temperatures sometimes reach the high 90's and remain there. Still, at that time, the Neiman-Marcus store stages a successful sale of wool sweaters. The management has been doing this for the past two years and sales have jumped 53% in that time. And in many other cities, the sale of lightweight wools has increased.

Why should this be? One answer is air-conditioning. We are living in the age of the fairly constant temperature. All over the nation, from Portland to Portland and from Duluth to Brownsville, our offices, theaters, restaurants, stores and homes are air-conditioned.

The idea has now spread to automobiles. The thermal change is a great blessing, but it also chills the surface of the body. The antidote to this is wool—lightweight wool sweaters, slacks, suits, separates.

Still, it seemed strange to me that millions of people would turn to wool in summertime so I engaged a few experts in conversation. They had the answers. I quote, "Wool's crimpy fibers trap insulating air in fabrics. This blocks out cold and drafts. Yet because wool is absorbent—it prevents that sticky feeling in warm, humid weather. And wool's light open-weave fabrics also provide cooling ventilation of the body when it's hot. So lightweight wools are great for heat waves—comfortable in cool interiors or on hot streets."

Since then, I've become wool conscious, and I am no longer surprised when I see every other man and woman wearing or carrying wool—or find my daughter Ginny wearing light wool slacks while pushing her twin children from shop to shop in the high heat of the afternoon. She seemed surprised to find that I was surprised that anyone would wear wool when even the birds were hiding in the protective foliage of the branches.

She mentioned another point which would not have occurred to me: colors, patterns and weights are so varied that any taste can find satisfaction in today's American-made wools.

Smart kid. Never at the head of her class, but always the best dressed. She learned early that "nothing measures up to wool."

## 如何在 90°的高温热浪中
让自己感觉像春天般温暖

这是专栏作家，《基督死的那一天》
《林肯被枪杀》的作者 Jim Bishop 发表的一个悖论

达拉斯的夏天十分炎热。7月份的时候，温度会高达 90°，并持续不下。不过，那时候，内曼·马库斯商店的羊毛毛衣销量却非常高。过去的两年中，销量直接上升到了 53%。在其他许多城市，轻薄羊毛的销量也在上升。

为什么会这样？答案是空调。我们现在生活在一个温度相当稳定的时代。全国各地，从波特兰到波特兰，从德卢斯到布朗斯威尔，我们的办公室、剧院、餐厅、商店和家中都装有空调。

如今汽车上也装有空调了。空调能控制温度，让人不再受热，是一个造福人类的发明，但同时也让体表感到冷。改变这种状况的方法就是羊毛——轻薄的羊毛毛衣、长裤、西装,两件套等。

不过，许多人在夏天穿羊毛制品，我依然觉得非常奇怪。所以我询问了几个专家。他们给出了答案。我引用一下，“羊毛卷曲的纤维，能把热空气锁在面料中，把冷风，冷空气挡在外面。然而，因为羊毛吸附性比较强——让人在湿热的天气，不再有那种黏热的感觉。和羊毛松散的纤维，能在燥热的天气里，让凉风通过。所以，轻薄的羊毛制品非常适合燥热的天气穿——在凉爽的室内或炎热的街头都可以。”

从那时起，我就知道羊毛的作用了。当我看到大街上许多男男女女都穿着，或拿着羊毛制品——或发现我女儿金妮穿着羊毛长裤，推她的双胞胎孩子在炎热的午后逛商店时，我不会再感到惊讶了。我之前不明白为什么鸟儿都要躲在树叶下乘凉时，人们却穿着羊毛感到惊讶时，这一点反而让她感到惊讶。

她还提到一点，这是我之前没想到的：现在美国的羊毛制品，有各种各样的颜色、图案和重量可供选择。

聪明的孩子。以前在班里从来没有考过前几名，但是穿着总是最好的。她知道“没什么能比得上羊毛。”
