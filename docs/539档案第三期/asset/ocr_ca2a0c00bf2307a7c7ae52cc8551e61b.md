# 52、舒适之奖

# The prize catch
in comfort

A revealing insight on clothes by a man whose leisure hours lead him to the great outdoors: C. P. Smith, President of American Airlines

![](bbox=[0.57, 0.2, 0.79, 0.46])

"I take my annual holiday on the Great Watshishu River in Quebec, Canada, fishing for Atlantic salmon. I have learned from long experience that wool clothing is needed to make it a pleasant holiday."

"Wool shirts or sweaters for the cool days. Wool underwear for the cold. Wool blankets for the night air. A wool greatcoat for an evening trip up the St. Lawrence. Wool pants to keep out the cold of icy water. Wool felt for the soles of the waders to avoid slipping on the rocks; wool socks to be worn inside them."

"Wool serves us well and makes life more pleasant and enjoyable. It would be difficult to do without the substantial benefits we derive from wool."

The reason Mr. Smith can count on wool's comfort and performance is that nature itself builds many unique features into the wool fiber.

For one thing, it provides every wool fiber with a waxy shield that repels rain and snow. And strangely enough, this shield also has tiny pores that absorb moisture vapor. That's why wool clothing protects you against the weather but never gives you that clammy, sealed-in feeling.

Nature also gives the wool fiber a crimp or "permanent wave." These crimped fibers form millions of tiny air pockets that provide insulation and keep you warm—protect you against temperature changes.

What's more, this same crimp gives the wool fiber unique elasticity. It can actually be stretched 30% and still return to shape. That's why wool gives you the freedom to swing a golf club or cast your line—without binding or restricting. And why wool clothing always looks so good, wears so well for so long.

Your own experience will tell you—for a quiet walk in the woods or a rugged camping trip—American-made wools give you unmatched comfort. Nothing measures up to wool.
