# The war
against “staph”

By Gene Wilburn,
Medical News Writer, Houston Chronicle

A virulent organism is rocking the medical world. Its name is Staphylococcus aureus, better known as “staph.” It recently came to public attention when it killed 16 babies in a Texas nursery. Actually, its victims can be any age, male or female. Its carriers don’t even have to be sick. Worse still, “staph” is alarmingly resistant to many antibiotics and other chemotherapeutic agents. “Staph” has developed into a nationwide community problem – a household problem. For it can be tracked into any home – the cleanest of homes. There need be no skin lesion for “staph” to enter the body. A crawling infant, a bather can pick it up from the cleanest looking floor, the whitest bathroom tiles.

That is why we should renew the old crusade for strict home disinfection. Since the bathroom can be a critical area for spread of disease germs, the bathroom cleanser should have the power to kill “staph” and other disease-bearing germs that may lurk in the bathroom. Published laboratory tests show that neither soap suds nor usual pine oil solu-

tions have the power to kill “staph” – that so-called “sanitizers” are not true germicides – that is, they may not kill all disease germs they touch.

One household product has proved to be an efficient germicide. Lysol Brand Disinfectant kills disease germs on contact – even “staph” and many deadly viruses. And, in laboratory tests, its anti-germ action was found to last 7 full days. Lysol disinfects from one cleaning to the next as nothing else can. Now in pine scent as well as regular, a bottle costs as little as 29¢.
