# 53、如何在 90°的高温热浪中让自己感觉像春天般温暖

# How to keep warm in a 90° heat wave

Report on a paradox by Jim Bishop, syndicated columnist and author of "The Day Christ Died" and "The Day Lincoln was Shot"

![](bbox=[0.56, 0.248, 0.735, 0.45])

Dallas in the summertime is hot. July temperatures sometimes reach the high 90's and remain there. Still, at that time, the Neiman-Marcus store stages a successful sale of wool sweaters. The management has been doing this for the past two years and sales have jumped 53% in that time. And in many other cities, the sale of lightweight wools has increased.

Why should this be? One answer is air-conditioning. We are living in the age of the fairly constant temperature. All over the nation, from Portland to Portland and from Duluth to Brownsville, our offices, theaters, restaurants, stores and homes are air-conditioned.

The idea has now spread to automobiles. The thermal change is a great blessing, but it also chills the surface of the body. The antidote to this is wool—lightweight wool sweaters, slacks, suits, separates.

Still, it seemed strange to me that millions of people would turn to wool in summertime so I engaged a few experts in conversation. They had the answers. I quote, "Wool's crimpy fibers trap insulating air in fabrics. This blocks out cold and drafts. Yet

because wool is absorbent—it prevents that sticky feeling in warm, humid weather. And wool's light open-weave fabrics also provide cooling ventilation of the body when it's hot. So lightweight wools are great for heat waves—comfortable in cool interiors or on hot streets."

Since then, I've become wool conscious, and I am no longer surprised when I see every other man and woman wearing or carrying wool—or find my daughter Ginny wearing light wool slacks while pushing her twin children from shop to shop in the high heat of the afternoon. She seemed surprised to find that I was surprised that anyone would wear wool when even the birds were hiding in the protective foliage of the branches.

She mentioned another point which would not have occurred to me: colors, patterns and weights are so varied that any taste can find satisfaction in today's American-made wools.

Smart kid. Never at the head of her class, but always the best dressed. She learned early that "nothing measures up to wool."
