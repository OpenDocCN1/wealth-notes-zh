# A private eye’s view of
TODAY’S YOUNG MOTHER

The trail starts outside a large supermarket. The mother is young — perhaps not even 22 or 23. With her is her little boy (or is it a girl—in those overalls you can never tell!).

She looks more like a high-school baby sitter than a mother — in her raincoat and loafers.

But don’t let her looks fool you. She’s as hep to the business of food enjoyment, of child feeding and bringing up, as would do credit to a woman twice her age. And why not! Much of her wisdom was absorbed from her mother, although they both might be surprised to hear it.

She knows all the kiss-the-cook dishes that are loved by her youngster. But she also knows her vitamin alphabet backwards and forwards and the importance of protein and minerals to her tooth-cutting and bone-growing son.

Watch her go quickly from section to section — selecting for best quality, nourishment and prices. Watch her stop at the soup section — and smartly pick a good variety of Campbell’s Soups.

She takes Tomato and Vegetable because they are family top favorites and because of the vitamins and minerals they bring to meals. She picks cream soups—Chicken or Cel- ery—because they taste so good, because they are so smooth. And she makes them with milk for the good food values that it adds.

And, of course, she makes sure she has at least one or two cans of Bean with Bacon, Beef, or Green Pea — for Bill and Bill, Jr., ask for seconds (and she knows these soups are body builders).

Today’s young mother isn’t so immature and uninformed as she looks.

Her market basket and her well-fed family tell the real story!
