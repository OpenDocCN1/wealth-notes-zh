# What to do when something gets in your eye

BECAUSE your eyes must be exposed in order to see, nature has provided them with special protection. Eyebrows (unless you have replaced them with pencil) and lashes help screen dirt from surrounding air. At the sudden approach of an object, your eyelids snap shut involuntarily — much faster than your thought-directed wink.

But now and then a wind-driven particle is even quicker, and gets in. Instantly, sensitive nerves of the eye signal for action. Tears gush, lids flutter in effort to flush away the intruder. Meanwhile, the intense discomfort makes all your other problems fade in comparison to the urgency of getting the accursed thing out.

Chances are your built-in, automatic systems will soon do the job; but should they fail, you may save yourself a lot of misery by following these few simple suggestions:

3. Twist a corner of a clean handkerchief to a small point. Often the offending particle can be lifted away with just a touch of this simple device. Do not attempt to use tweezers or any other sharp or hard instrument.

A particle may be hard to find because it clings to the inner side of the upper lid. Grasp the lashes of the upper lid, lift it down over the lower lid and release it so that the lower lid's lashes brush beneath the upper lid. This ready-made whiskbroom is surprisingly effective.

4. If, after trying these simple methods, you still feel that something is in your eye, get to an eye doctor as soon as you can.

A few drops of Murine will help wash away minute particles like powder or ashes. Always use Murine in both eyes after you have removed anything from either eye. Murine is isotonic in respect to tears, which means that it blends perfectly with the natural fluids of your eyes. You'll find its gentle, soothing effect comforting and refreshing.

+   1. Don't rub your eye! Rubbing can cause the object in your eye to scratch, or become embedded in the lid or surface of the eye. Embedded particles which may seem to "go away" can result in serious later infection.

2. Try to locate the object with a mirror, rolling the eye up, down and to each side.
